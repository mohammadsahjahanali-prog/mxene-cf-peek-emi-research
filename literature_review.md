# Literature Review: MXene/Carbon Fiber Thermoplastic Composites for EMI Shielding
1. **Shahzad, F., Alhabeb, M., Hatter, C. B., Anasori, B., Hong, S. M., Koo, C. M., & Gogotsi, Y. (2016). *Electromagnetic interference shielding with 2D transition metal carbides (MXenes)*. Science, 353(6304), 1137–1140.**
   This foundational study demonstrates that two-dimensional titanium carbide ($\text{Ti}_3\text{C}_2\text{T}_x$) MXene films exhibit metallic electrical conductivity and internal multiple-reflection mechanisms that yield exceptional electromagnetic interference shielding effectiveness (EMI SE) at micrometer thicknesses. The authors show that MXene-polymer composites achieve commercial shielding benchmarks ($>20\text{ dB}$) at substantially lower filler loadings than traditional carbon-based nanomaterials.
2. **Wang, L., Chen, L., Song, P., Liang, C., Lu, Y., Qiu, H., Zhang, Y., Kong, J., & Gu, J. (2019). *Fabrication on the annealed $\text{Ti}_3\text{C}_2\text{T}_x$ MXene/Epoxy nanocomposites for electromagnetic interference shielding application*. Composites Part B: Engineering, 171, 111–118.**
   The authors investigate the construction of three-dimensional conductive networks by incorporating thermally annealed $\text{Ti}_3\text{C}_2\text{T}_x$ MXene into polymer matrices. Thermal annealing removes surface terminal groups, boosting electrical conductivity and shifting the dominant shielding mechanism from reflection toward absorption-dominated attenuation while maintaining mechanical integrity.
3. **Zhan, Y., Oliviero, M., Wang, J., Sorrentino, A., Buonocore, G. G., & Lavorgna, M. (2019). *Enhancing the EMI shielding of natural rubber-based supercritical $\text{CO}_2$ foams by exploiting their porous morphology and segregated carbon nanotube networks*. Nanoscale, 11(3), 1011–1020.**
   This work reveals how cellular porosity and segregated conductive networks synergistically trap incident electromagnetic waves via internal scattering. Reducing composite bulk density through controlled micro-void architecture increases specific shielding effectiveness ($\text{SSE/t}$), which is critical for lightweight aerospace and portable electronic enclosures.
4. **Babu, K., Das, O., Shanmugam, V., Mensah, R. A., Försth, M., Sas, G., & Hedenqvist, M. S. (2022). *Fire behavior of 3D-printed polymeric composites and machine learning-based property prediction*. Polymers, 14(7), 1345.**
   The authors demonstrate how high-performance thermoplastics like Polyether Ether Ketone (PEEK) reinforced with carbon fibers provide superior thermal stability and structural stiffness for aerospace applications. Integrating ensemble machine learning algorithms enables rapid prediction of multifunctional composite properties across complex processing regimes without exhaustive physical prototyping.
